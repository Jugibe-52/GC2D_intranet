\section{Polynomial-path formulation}

Let $(c_i,b_i)_{i=1}^4$ be four-point Gauss--Legendre quadrature on $[0,1]$.
For the first two orthonormal shifted Legendre polynomials,
\[
P_0(c)=1,\qquad P_1(c)=\sqrt3(2c-1),
\]
define
\[
I_{i0}=c_i,\qquad I_{i1}=\sqrt3(c_i^2-c_i).
\]
HBVM(4,2) solves for two vector coefficients $\gamma_0,\gamma_1$:
\begin{align}
Y_i&=y_n+h\sum_{j=0}^1I_{ij}\gamma_j,\\
R_j(\gamma)&=\gamma_j-
\sum_{i=1}^4b_iP_j(c_i)f(t_n+c_ih,Y_i)=0.
\end{align}
The accepted state is
\[
y_{n+1}=y_n+h\sum_{i=1}^4b_i f(t_n+c_ih,Y_i).
\]
Equivalently, the Runge--Kutta matrix is
$A=\mathcal I_2\mathcal P_2^T\Omega_q$ and has rank two.  The nonlinear
unknown therefore has twice the state dimension, although four stages evaluate
the line integral.

\section{Order and energy property}

With polynomial degree $s=2$, the method has classical order $2s=4$.
Four-point Gauss quadrature has degree seven.  For an autonomous polynomial
Hamiltonian of degree $\nu$, exact polynomial energy conservation follows when
$k\geq\nu s/2$; hence HBVM(4,2) covers degrees through four.  For a general
smooth Hamiltonian the quadrature error makes energy conservation approximate,
with an error that decreases rapidly with the step and quadrature accuracy.

HBVM$(k,s)$ is generally not symplectic when $k>s$.  Thus HBVM(4,2) must not be
labeled symplectic by construction; map-Jacobian diagnostics measure its
canonical defect independently of its energy behavior.

\section{Nonlinear configurations}

Newton acts on the reduced residual $(R_0,R_1)$. Pass
\texttt{solver=NewtonSolver(...)}; its \texttt{jacobian\_method} selects
differentiation. \texttt{analytic} uses the dense or particle-block Jacobian supplied by execution;
\texttt{finite\_difference} differentiates stage fields; \texttt{auto} uses the
supplied derivative when available. The solver owns tolerances and iteration
limits. HBVM defaults to \texttt{backtracking=True}, which shortens corrections
when the full Newton update increases the residual. The stopping scale is
\[
\varepsilon_{\rm abs}+\varepsilon_{\rm rel}
\max(1,\lVert y_n\rVert_\infty).
\]

An optional \texttt{EnergyObserver} stores the physical Hamiltonian and the
passive balance $H+\kappa-H(t_0,z_0)$ at saved times. For explicitly
time-dependent systems this does not claim conservation of physical $H$.

\section{Physical accepted states and passive energy tracking}
For $N$ particles with $d$ physical components, every accepted snapshot contains
$Z\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}$ and one scalar time $t$. An optional energy observer
owns separate physical momentum $\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.
Thus GC accepted vectors have $2N$
components and FC vectors have $4N$, whether energy is tracked or not.
A doubled method prepares temporary $(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2dN}$ copies,
allows them to separate, and projects to one physical result before acceptance.
The representation stores only the physical size and never owns a clock,
momentum, physical coordinate declaration, dynamics or solver.

The normalized passive balance is
\[
\dot\kappa=-\partial_tH(t,Z),\qquad \kappa(t_0)=0,\qquad
\Delta K(t)=H(t,Z(t))+\kappa(t)-H(t_0,Z_0).
\]
GC split-map contributions are summed in temporary work and divided by two
to obtain physical momentum. Tracking leaves physical stages, nonlinear
stopping scales and adaptive acceptance unchanged. An autonomous Hamiltonian
supplies an explicit zero momentum derivative.

Hairer's constraint is solely $u-v=0$: the reduced multiplier has $2N$ entries
and ABBA's simultaneous spatial solve has $6N$ unknowns. Neither $t$ nor
$\kappa$ is projected. The former eight-component projection is retired.
Dynamics declare component order and position/velocity roles through immutable
structural data; initial conditions and results retain those data independently.
Execution injects $f(t,Z)$ and optional physical derivatives into a fresh method
run. Methods own physical stages and projection equations, without retaining the
dynamics object. Nonlinear tolerances, iteration limits, differentiation and
iteration callbacks belong to the configured algebraic solver. Generic doubled
copy operations live in \path{src/formulations/state.py}; classical, HBVM and
adaptive methods need no state formulation.

Methods optionally return retained stage quadrature or a dense interpolant.
The energy observer integrates momentum independently, using Hamiltonian
functions supplied by execution. Controllers keep only physical accepted states;
compiled JAX loops carry the observer accumulator separately.
Observers receive physical states. Energy histories in \texttt{States.diagnostics}
retain their existing labels; \texttt{extended\_time} has shape
$(N,n_{\rm samples})$ and is broadcast from scalar sample times.
Fixed shadow samples leave accepted states and counters unchanged. Adaptive
quadrature uses accepted dense output and physical-only error control.



The four converged stage states and Gauss weights integrate the passive momentum. Its value never enters the Legendre coefficient solve.


\section{Optional JAX execution}

The optional JAX driver executes the same map in double precision on one
selected CPU or GPU, batching particles inside a compiled sequential time loop.
It preserves coefficients, physical tolerance scaling, signed stage times,
passive quadrature, independent shortened output maps and convergence failures.
Newton uses the supplied particle-block structure. Both routes return the same
physical solution layout; floating-point reassociation can change round-off
without changing the designed order or geometric properties. First-call
compilation is separate from warmed timings, which include transfers and output.
The shared execution contract specifies callbacks and device scope.
