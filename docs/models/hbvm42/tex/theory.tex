 \vspace{0.35em}{\large Rank-two polynomial path with four Gauss nodes}

\section{Polynomial-path formulation}

Let $(c_i,b_i)_{i=1}^4$ be four-point Gauss--Legendre quadrature on $[0,1]$.
For the first two orthonormal shifted Legendre polynomials,
\[
P_0(c)=1,\qquad P_1(c)=\sqrt3(2c-1),
\]
define
\[
I_{i0}=c_i,\qquad I_{i1}=\sqrt3(c_i^2-c_i).
\]
HBVM(4,2) solves for two vector coefficients $\gamma_0,\gamma_1$:
\begin{align}
Y_i&=y_n+h\sum_{j=0}^1I_{ij}\gamma_j,\\
R_j(\gamma)&=\gamma_j-
\sum_{i=1}^4b_iP_j(c_i)f(t_n+c_ih,Y_i)=0.
\end{align}
The accepted state is
\[
y_{n+1}=y_n+h\sum_{i=1}^4b_i f(t_n+c_ih,Y_i).
\]
Equivalently, the Runge--Kutta matrix is
$A=\mathcal I_2\mathcal P_2^T\Omega_q$ and has rank two.  The nonlinear
unknown therefore has twice the state dimension, although four stages evaluate
the line integral.

\section{Order and energy property}

With polynomial degree $s=2$, the method has classical order $2s=4$.
Four-point Gauss quadrature has degree seven.  For an autonomous polynomial
Hamiltonian of degree $\nu$, exact polynomial energy conservation follows when
$k\geq\nu s/2$; hence HBVM(4,2) covers degrees through four.  For a general
smooth Hamiltonian the quadrature error makes energy conservation approximate,
with an error that decreases rapidly with the step and quadrature accuracy.

HBVM$(k,s)$ is generally not symplectic when $k>s$.  Thus HBVM(4,2) must not be
labeled symplectic by construction; map-Jacobian diagnostics measure its
canonical defect independently of its energy behavior.

\section{Nonlinear configurations}

Newton acts on the reduced residual $(R_0,R_1)$. Pass
\texttt{solver=NewtonSolver(...)}; its \texttt{jacobian\_method} selects
differentiation. \texttt{analytic} uses the dense or particle-block Jacobian supplied by execution;
\texttt{finite\_difference} differentiates stage fields; \texttt{auto} uses the
supplied derivative when available. The solver owns tolerances and iteration
limits. HBVM defaults to \texttt{backtracking=True}, which shortens corrections
when the full Newton update increases the residual. The stopping scale is
\[
\varepsilon_{\rm abs}+\varepsilon_{\rm rel}
\max(1,\lVert y_n\rVert_\infty).
\]

The generic \texttt{EnergyObserver} evaluates physical $H$ and analytic
positive $\partial_t H$ at accepted endpoints and requested physical samples.
It records energy differences without integrating momentum or using internal
stages. A historical $H+\kappa$ study must explicitly request the separate
stage-based diagnostic. Physical trajectories and solver equations are unchanged.

\section{Physical state and endpoint energy observations}
Accepted states contain one physical vector $Z\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}$ and scalar time.
GC vectors have $2N$ entries and FC vectors have $4N$; doubled workspaces are
transient. InitialConditions supplies values and layout during preparation only.
The numerical base retains functions, metadata, run status and copy count;
execution owns the initial snapshot and output schedule. Supplied Jacobians are
independent particle blocks $(N,d,d)$; finite differences use dense matrices.

The generic \texttt{EnergyObserver} evaluates only
\[
 H_n=H(t_n,Z_n),\quad H_{n+1}=H(t_{n+1},Z_{n+1}),\quad
 (\partial_tH)_n,\quad (\partial_tH)_{n+1},\quad
 \Delta H_n=H_{n+1}-H_n.
\]
It also evaluates these physical functions at requested saved samples, without
consuming internal stages or integrating a momentum coordinate. The derivative
is positive, analytic and partial at fixed state. GC differentiates its normalized
Hamiltonian potential with \texttt{dt=1}; spatial splines and Fourier time factors
retain their original scaling. This is not the secant $\Delta H_n/h$.
Variation of a time-dependent Hamiltonian is not a numerical conservation error.

Specialized historical generalized-energy studies separately reconstruct
$\dot\kappa=-\partial_tH$ from retained numerical stages. Their signed weights,
physical normalization and archived diagnostics keep their original meaning.
They are not part of generic energy observation. Host and JAX execution share
the fixed numerical map and physical-only accepted state. JAX evaluates endpoint
energy on the device without a momentum accumulator.

\section{Optional JAX execution}

The optional JAX driver executes the same map in double precision on one
selected CPU or GPU, batching particles inside a compiled sequential time loop.
It preserves coefficients, physical tolerance scaling, signed stage times,
passive quadrature, independent shortened output maps and convergence failures.
Newton uses the supplied particle-block structure. Both routes return the same
physical solution layout; floating-point reassociation can change round-off
without changing the designed order or geometric properties. First-call
compilation is separate from warmed timings, which include transfers and output.
The shared execution contract specifies callbacks and device scope.
