\section*{Implicit midpoint}
For $z'=f(t,z)$ solve one stage equation
\[
Y=z_n+\frac h2 f(t_n+h/2,Y),\qquad z_{n+1}=z_n+h f(t_n+h/2,Y).
\]
The tableau is $A=(1/2)$, $b=(1)$, $c=(1/2)$.
The order-two conditions $\sum_i b_i=1$ and $\sum_i b_ic_i=1/2$ hold.
For sufficiently smooth $f$, the local truncation error is $O(h^3)$ and global error is $O(h^2)$ on bounded time intervals under the usual stability assumptions.
The Newton stage residual is $R(Y)=Y-z_n-hf(t_n+h/2,Y)/2$ with Jacobian $I-hD_zf/2$.
The step must admit the intended local solution; smoothness alone does not ensure a unique stage for arbitrary $h$.
One stage can require multiple nonlinear corrections and field evaluations.

The Runge--Kutta symplecticity condition $b_i a_{ij}+b_j a_{ji}=b_i b_j$ reduces to $1/2+1/2=1$.
With an exact stage solution the method is symplectic for canonical Hamiltonian systems and symmetric under exchanging endpoints and replacing $h$ by $-h$.
For any preserved quadratic invariant $Q(z)=z^TCz$, $C=C^T$, the difference is
$Q(z_{n+1})-Q(z_n)=2hY^TCf(t_n+h/2,Y)=0$.
Finite algebraic tolerance adds a defect; neither symplecticity nor quadratic conservation implies preservation of a general time-dependent Hamiltonian.

\section*{Implementation boundary}
The named recipe inherits the shared Runge--Kutta stage algorithm and declares only its fixed tableau and order.
Methods receive a field and independent initial conditions; physical dynamics and passive energy observation remain outside the step.
Execution owns fixed-step scheduling, output sampling and NumPy/JAX selection.
