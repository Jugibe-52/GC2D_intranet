\section{Purpose and model boundary}

The ABBA model is a family of integrators built from the same explicit
$A$--$B$--$B$--$A$ map on two copies of a physical state. A public class
selects the order and projection recipe;
configuration controls select the
projection equations, nonlinear solver, state extension, and optional physical
energy tracking.  These choices are configurations of a model, not additional
model identities.

The family has four public method classes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{ABBA2Midpoint} and \texttt{ABBA2Implicit};
\item \texttt{ABBA4Implicit}, with one outer projection; and
\item \texttt{ABBA6Implicit}.
\end{itemize}
Their focused theory documents live in the sibling model directories.

\section{Duplicated-space ABBA map}

Let $\dot z=f(t,z)$ with $z\in\R^m$.  Duplicate the state as $(u,v)$ and set
$s=h/2$.  One signed endpoint-time step beginning at $t$ is
\begin{align}
 u_1 &= u_0+s f(t,v_0), &
 v_1 &= v_0+s f(t,u_1),\\
 v_f &= v_1+s f(t+h,u_1), &
 u_f &= u_1+s f(t+h,v_f).
\end{align}
We denote this map by $\Psi_{h,t}(u_0,v_0)=(u_f,v_f)$.  Its reverse is
$\Psi_{-h,t+h}$, so the endpoint-time convention is essential for
non-autonomous reversibility.  When the split system is Hamiltonian, each
line is a shear and the composition is symplectic on the duplicated space.

With
\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}I\\I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}I&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 G=\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\end{pmatrix},\qquad N=G^T,
\]
$Ez$ embeds the physical diagonal, $P$ takes its arithmetic mean, and
$G(u,v)=u-v$ measures copy separation.

\section{Projection choices}

The midpoint method accepts $P\Psi_{h,t}(Ez)$ directly.  The implicit methods
use Hairer's symmetric projection.  In the reduced formulation a multiplier
$\mu\in\R^m$ displaces the input and corrects the output:
\begin{align}
 \widehat Y &= Ez+N\mu, & M&=\Psi_{h,t}(\widehat Y),\\
 r(\mu)&=GM+2\mu=0, & z^+&=P(M+N\mu).
\end{align}
If $J_\Psi=D\Psi_{h,t}(\widehat Y)$, Newton uses
\[
 D_\mu r=GJ_\Psi N+2I.
\]

The simultaneous formulation solves for the corrected doubled output $Y$ and
$\mu$ together:
\[
 F(Y,\mu)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 Y-N\mu-\Psi_{h,t}(Ez+N\mu)\\GY
 \end{pmatrix}=0.
\]
Eliminating $Y$ gives the reduced equation.  Thus both formulations define
the same ideal projected map when they converge to the same root.  Newton uses
the exact residual Jacobian; good Broyden replaces repeated Jacobian assembly
by rank-one secant updates without changing the defining residual.

\section{Canonical configuration space}
ABBA2, ABBA4 and ABBA6 select different unprojected compositions and share one outer projection. Energy tracking is optional and passive.
Signed accepted shear-stage traces update the passive momentum independently of the spatial root solve.
The common state conventions are specified below.

\section{Order by symmetric composition}

The unprojected ABBA2 map is a symmetric method of order two in doubled
space. Higher order is obtained by palindromic signed compositions, followed
by one symmetric projection around the complete result.  ABBA4 uses the triple jump
$(\gamma,\delta,\gamma)$ with
\[
 \gamma=\frac{1}{2-\sqrt[3]{2}},\qquad
 \delta=-\frac{\sqrt[3]{2}}{2-\sqrt[3]{2}},
 \quad 2\gamma+\delta=1,\quad 2\gamma^3+\delta^3=0.
\]
ABBA6 uses seven Yoshida coefficients whose first, third, and fifth odd
moments have the required cancellations. These moment checks alone are not
a full sixth-order proof; the Yoshida solution also satisfies the mixed
commutator condition. Negative coefficients are intrinsic
to these real compositions, so the dynamics must be defined at all internal
signed times.

The former three-projection fourth-order implementation has been removed.
ABBA4 and ABBA6 now both have one outer projection. The retired
single-projection ABBA4 factory is no longer an importable interface.

\section{Finite solves and geometric claims}

Symmetry, formal order, and symplecticity concern the ideal map obtained from
the exact projection root.  A finite stopping tolerance and floating-point
arithmetic perturb that map.  Consequently, the implementation records
iterations, residual evaluations, final residual norms, tolerances, and
projection multipliers.  Step observers expose the accepted map so measured
Jacobian defects can be distinguished from ideal algebraic properties.

\section{Implementation correspondence}
The existing ABBA classes select their recipes in the \texttt{order*.py}
modules under \path{src/methods/extended/}. Their common run
operations live in \texttt{abba.py}; the spatial engine is shared with BM4.

The public names, effective numerical defaults and exports from \texttt{simulation}
remain unchanged. Internal imports use the defining modules; old import
adapters have been removed. The ordinary implicit path is \texttt{advance}, \texttt{solve\_projection},
\texttt{compose}, then \texttt{direct\_map} or \texttt{adjoint\_map}.
Arithmetic midpoint variants use one explicit average instead of a nonlinear
projection. Adjoint stages evaluate at the start of their signed substep;
direct stages evaluate at its end.

Newton and Broyden share the spatial equations and convergence services.
Exact particle-block tangents, passive energy and optional observation use
retained shear inputs; energy is evaluated only after accepting the spatial
map, with the existing factor $1/2$ for physical $\kappa$. Time and momentum
never enter the spatial root. The new internal family name does not enable
the retired full time/momentum projection.

Current module responsibilities and diagrams are documented in
\path{docs/models/extended/simulation/extended-simulation-architecture.md}.

\section{Common integration lifecycle}
The executor initializes a fresh numerical instance through
\texttt{IntegrationMethod.new\_run} and starts the shared integration driver.
Implicit orders share a family base.
The fixed controller supplies accepted intervals and independent shadow
samples; the coordinator owns progress, collection and event dispatch.
Only sampled states and accepted metric rows are retained by the collector.

\section{Physical accepted states and passive energy tracking}
For $N$ particles with $d$ physical components, every accepted snapshot contains
$Z\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}$ and one scalar time $t$. An optional energy observer
owns separate physical momentum $\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.
Thus GC accepted vectors have $2N$
components and FC vectors have $4N$, whether energy is tracked or not.
A doubled method prepares temporary $(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2dN}$ copies,
allows them to separate, and projects to one physical result before acceptance.
The representation stores only the physical size and never owns a clock,
momentum, physical coordinate declaration, dynamics or solver.

The normalized passive balance is
\[
\dot\kappa=-\partial_tH(t,Z),\qquad \kappa(t_0)=0,\qquad
\Delta K(t)=H(t,Z(t))+\kappa(t)-H(t_0,Z_0).
\]
GC split-map contributions are summed in temporary work and divided by two
to obtain physical momentum. Tracking leaves physical stages, nonlinear
stopping scales and adaptive acceptance unchanged. An autonomous Hamiltonian
supplies an explicit zero momentum derivative.

Hairer's constraint is solely $u-v=0$: the reduced multiplier has $2N$ entries
and ABBA's simultaneous spatial solve has $6N$ unknowns. Neither $t$ nor
$\kappa$ is projected. The former eight-component projection is retired.
Dynamics declare component order and position/velocity roles through immutable
structural data; initial conditions and results retain those data independently.
Execution injects $f(t,Z)$ and optional physical derivatives into a fresh method
run. Methods own physical stages and projection equations, without retaining the
dynamics object. Nonlinear tolerances, iteration limits, differentiation and
iteration callbacks belong to the configured algebraic solver. Generic doubled
copy operations live in \path{src/formulations/state.py}; classical, HBVM and
adaptive methods need no state formulation.

Methods optionally return retained stage quadrature or a dense interpolant.
The energy observer integrates momentum independently, using Hamiltonian
functions supplied by execution. Controllers keep only physical accepted states;
compiled JAX loops carry the observer accumulator separately.
Observers receive physical states. Energy histories in \texttt{States.diagnostics}
retain their existing labels; \texttt{extended\_time} has shape
$(N,n_{\rm samples})$ and is broadcast from scalar sample times.
Fixed shadow samples leave accepted states and counters unchanged. Adaptive
quadrature uses accepted dense output and physical-only error control.
