\section{Purpose and model boundary}

The ABBA model is a family of integrators built from the same explicit
$A$--$B$--$B$--$A$ map on two copies of a physical state. A public class
selects the order and projection recipe;
configuration controls select the
projection equations, nonlinear solver, state extension, and optional physical
energy tracking.  These choices are configurations of a model, not additional
model identities.

The family has four public method classes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{ABBA2Midpoint} and \texttt{ABBA2Implicit};
\item \texttt{ABBA4Implicit}, with one outer projection; and
\item \texttt{ABBA6Implicit}.
\end{itemize}
Their focused theory documents live in the sibling model directories.

\section{Duplicated-space ABBA map}

Let $\dot z=f(t,z)$ with $z\in\R^m$.  Duplicate the state as $(u,v)$ and set
$s=h/2$.  One signed endpoint-time step beginning at $t$ is
\begin{align}
 u_1 &= u_0+s f(t,v_0), &
 v_1 &= v_0+s f(t,u_1),\\
 v_f &= v_1+s f(t+h,u_1), &
 u_f &= u_1+s f(t+h,v_f).
\end{align}
We denote this map by $\Psi_{h,t}(u_0,v_0)=(u_f,v_f)$.  Its reverse is
$\Psi_{-h,t+h}$, so the endpoint-time convention is essential for
non-autonomous reversibility.  When the split system is Hamiltonian, each
line is a shear and the composition is symplectic on the duplicated space.

With
\[
 E=\begin{pmatrix}I\\I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 P=\tfrac12\begin{pmatrix}I&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 G=\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\end{pmatrix},\qquad N=G^T,
\]
$Ez$ embeds the physical diagonal, $P$ takes its arithmetic mean, and
$G(u,v)=u-v$ measures copy separation.

\section{Projection choices}

The midpoint method accepts $P\Psi_{h,t}(Ez)$ directly.  The implicit methods
use Hairer's symmetric projection.  In the reduced formulation a multiplier
$\mu\in\R^m$ displaces the input and corrects the output:
\begin{align}
 \widehat Y &= Ez+N\mu, & M&=\Psi_{h,t}(\widehat Y),\\
 r(\mu)&=GM+2\mu=0, & z^+&=P(M+N\mu).
\end{align}
If $J_\Psi=D\Psi_{h,t}(\widehat Y)$, Newton uses
\[
 D_\mu r=GJ_\Psi N+2I.
\]

The simultaneous formulation solves for the corrected doubled output $Y$ and
$\mu$ together:
\[
 F(Y,\mu)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 Y-N\mu-\Psi_{h,t}(Ez+N\mu)\\GY
 \end{pmatrix}=0.
\]
Eliminating $Y$ gives the reduced equation.  Thus both formulations define
the same ideal projected map when they converge to the same root.  Newton uses
the exact residual Jacobian; good Broyden replaces repeated Jacobian assembly
by rank-one secant updates without changing the defining residual.

The state-extension values are \texttt{physical} and
\texttt{fully\_extended}; physical execution may disable or enable energy
tracking, while fully extended execution always enables it.  Thus the three
canonical normalized strategies are
$(\texttt{physical},\texttt{False})$,
$(\texttt{physical},\texttt{True})$, and
$(\texttt{fully\_extended},\texttt{True})$.  \texttt{ABBA2Implicit} and
\texttt{ABBA6Implicit} each admit $2\times2\times3=12$ canonical
configurations, including the single outer-projection \texttt{ABBA4Implicit}.  \texttt{ABBA2Midpoint} has no nonlinear equations and
supports the same three strategies.  The family therefore contains
$12+12+12+3=39$ canonical public configurations while retaining four method
classes.

For one guiding-centre particle, the state dimensions are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcccc@{}}
\toprule
Extension and tracking & accepted & split & reduced unknown & simultaneous unknown\\
\midrule
physical, off & $2$ & $4$ & $2$ & $6$\\
physical, on & $2$ & $4$ & $2$ & $6$\\
fully extended, on & $4$ & $8$ & $4$ & $12$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The simultaneous vector is nonlinear-solver workspace, not a trajectory
state.  Physical energy tracking reuses the accepted ABBA stages to advance
one auxiliary normalized momentum $\kappa=k/2$ per particle.  The update is
triangular: it does not feed back into $z$ and does not change the accepted,
split, observer, or nonlinear-workspace dimensions.  Fully extended mode
instead duplicates $Z=(z,t,k)$ and projects the complete autonomous state.

\section{Order by symmetric composition}

The projected ABBA2 map is a symmetric method of order two.  Higher order is
obtained by palindromic signed compositions.  ABBA4 uses the triple jump
$(\gamma,\delta,\gamma)$ with
\[
 \gamma=\frac{1}{2-\sqrt[3]{2}},\qquad
 \delta=-\frac{\sqrt[3]{2}}{2-\sqrt[3]{2}},
 \quad 2\gamma+\delta=1,\quad 2\gamma^3+\delta^3=0.
\]
ABBA6 uses seven Yoshida coefficients whose first, third, and fifth odd
moments have the required cancellations.  Negative coefficients are intrinsic
to these real compositions, so the dynamics must be defined at all internal
signed times.

The former three-projection fourth-order implementation has been removed.
The current default has one projection, and legacy single-projection names
refer to that same map.

\section{Finite solves and geometric claims}

Symmetry, formal order, and symplecticity concern the ideal map obtained from
the exact projection root.  A finite stopping tolerance and floating-point
arithmetic perturb that map.  Consequently, the implementation records
iterations, residual evaluations, final residual norms, tolerances, and
projection multipliers.  Step observers expose the accepted map so measured
Jacobian defects can be distinguished from ideal algebraic properties.

\section{Implementation correspondence}

The common stage kernel is in \texttt{src/simulation/methods/abba/\_core.py};
the two projection formulations are in \texttt{projection\_reduced.py} and
\texttt{projection\_simultaneous.py}; configuration validation is in
\texttt{\_configuration.py}; and the four public classes are implemented by
the corresponding \texttt{order*.py} modules. The private exterior-projection
ABBA4 kernel is in \texttt{projection\_outer.py}; the old single-projection
module now provides compatibility imports. The architecture document in
\texttt{../simulation/} is the authoritative runtime inventory.


\section{Common integration lifecycle}
All four ABBA classes own initialization, advance, observation and history
export. They inherit \texttt{integrate} from \texttt{IntegrationMethod}, which
creates a fresh instance of the same numerical class and initializes its
resources once. The three implicit orders share these operations in their
family base class. No separate method context or callback record is used.
The fixed controller supplies accepted intervals and independent shadow
samples; the common coordinator owns progress, collection and event dispatch.
\texttt{IntegrationCollector} stores saved internal states and accepted metric
rows; family-specific numerical traces are not retained as run history.
DOP853 and Radau supply adaptive controllers to the same global coordinator.
ABBA retains fixed-step numerical control.
