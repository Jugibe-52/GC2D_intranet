# Geometric defects of midpoint projection

The current step/horizon experiment for thesis section 4.8 is documented in [the accumulated sweep](midpoint-geometry-sweep.md). This page preserves the original 2026-11-18 local and 100-cycle protocol and its results.

This dedicated study supports thesis section 4.9 by measuring the symplecticity and time-symmetry defects of ABBA2Midpoint and BM4Midpoint. ABBA2Implicit and BM4Implicit provide symmetric-projection controls. It uses the reflected potential and the 84 initial positions of `main-20261117-rho-div20`, at normalized gyro-radius 0.015. The original campaign and its trajectories are unchanged; these new calculations answer a separate geometric question.

For the complete physical step Phi at time t, including final averaging, retain the signed determinant defect `det(D Phi)-1` and the infinity norm of `Phi(-h,t+h,Phi(h,t,z))-z`. Each map receives a physical point and reconstructs its own copies; passing the discarded copies into the reverse step would test a different algorithm. In two spatial dimensions, unit determinant is exactly the symplectic condition. The reverse test must begin at t+h because the potential depends on time.

The local study uses the same 84 points at forcing phases 0, 1/4, 1/2 and 3/4, with 20, 40, 80 and 160 fixed steps per cycle. Forty additional probes on both sides of the reflected joins, on the joins themselves and at the four corners are saved separately and do not change the 84-particle statistics. Preserve unwrapped coordinates. Analytic field Jacobians propagate derivatives through the actual elementary composition. With `e=(u-v)/2` at its end, compare `det(D Phi)-1` with `det(D e)` and retain the residual of that identity. The size of the copy separation alone does not determine the symplectic defect. A separate centered-difference audit checks the analytic complete-map tangent at particle IDs 1, 41 and 84, using perturbations from 1e-4 to 1e-7; this audit is a derivative check, not the estimator used for the reported small defects.

Calibration uses the oscillator `H=(x²+y²)/2` and the smooth nonlinear Hamiltonian `H=(x²+y²)/2+x²y²/4`, at steps `h=2^-k` for `k=0,...,6`. The oscillator gives the exact ABBA2 determinant defect `h^6/64`; its return displacement is this value times the initial infinity norm. Projection controls use equal absolute and relative tolerances of 1e-8, 1e-11 and 1e-14, with at most 40 Newton corrections. Their return displacement is measured from the actual algorithm, whereas their tangent differentiates the projection equations at the computed root, not the solver stopping rule. A small determinant defect for that tangent alone therefore does not establish the structure of the tolerance-limited algorithm. Medians, 95th percentiles and maxima summarize each common-point sample. Slopes from log step versus log defect describe the observed range only: roundoff, solver tolerances and the reflected spline's finite regularity can prevent recovery of a smooth-problem asymptotic order. Small measured defects do not establish exact symplecticity or symmetry.

The accumulation study uses 40 fixed steps per cycle for 10 and 100 cycles. Summing `log(abs(det(D Phi)))` tracks the area factor of the composed physical map when all step determinants are nonzero. A long forward/backward displacement also contains perturbation amplification and rounding; it is complementary to the local symmetry defect, not a direct estimate of its order. The time-dependent physical Hamiltonian is not used as a conservation-error surrogate.
