# Eight-method JAX timings

The study compares RK2, ImplicitMidpoint, RK4, GaussLegendre4, ABBA2Midpoint, ABBA2Implicit, BM4Midpoint and BM4Implicit. It uses the original 40 particles introduced in appendix C.2, verified as the unchanged subset of the reference campaign, the reflected measured potential at normalized rho=0.015, 500 forcing cycles, 40 fixed steps and ten saved samples per cycle. Each invocation produces 20,000 integration steps and 5,001 saved states per particle. This dedicated runtime study does not alter the 84-particle, 5,000-cycle reference campaign.

The [calculation notebook](../../notebooks/04_02_cout_calcul/calcul.ipynb) executes the eight methods sequentially in one Modal CPU container, with two requested/maximum cores and 4096/8192 MiB requested/maximum memory. JAX uses float64. All implicit methods retain analytic Newton Jacobians, absolute tolerance 1e-12, relative tolerance 1e-11 and at most 40 corrections. The four rounds contain one first invocation and three cached repetitions per method: 32 complete integrations, each of 500 cycles. Method order reverses between successive rounds. CPU sharing outside the container is not controlled, and the processor model may be hidden by Modal.

Timing covers `Execution.run`: preparation, integration, JAX synchronization and construction of host results. It excludes input restoration, container startup, audit checks, file/network transfers and figures. The first invocation includes compilation and other first-use overhead; it is not an isolated measurement of compilation. Persistent JAX caching is disabled, and the in-process integration cache is cleared before the first round. Every first call must register exactly one miss, and every later call exactly one hit. Completed, finite float64 states, aligned outputs, exact step counts and bitwise repeatability are checked outside timing. No inter-particle or inter-method distances, energy observations or optional step histories are computed. These choices follow the [JAX timing guidance](https://docs.jax.dev/en/latest/benchmarking.html).

The [visualization notebook](../../notebooks/04_02_cout_calcul/visualisation.ipynb) runs independently in Colab or Jupyter with NumPy and Matplotlib and is [published on Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/drive/1_AFGcTjOxlplLJdRxEfFquAkIQ_LiToS). Its loader downloads only `study.json`, `timings.json` and a dedicated publication manifest over public HTTPS. It checks the manifest against a pinned SHA-256 digest, checks both files against the manifest, and validates all 32 timing records before deriving the statistics. This path needs no GC2D checkout, JAX, rclone or credentials and performs no integrations. It reports the original trajectory-completeness and determinism audits as recorded provenance; it does not repeat those audits or download the solution archives. The published notebook retains the statistics and French figure for viewing without execution.

The figure shows cached medians and minimum–maximum ranges, with first-call times as separate markers. Both axes are logarithmic, with the same fixed denominator for every marker and range endpoint. The figure labels remain French. The thesis presents the combined chart and its analysis without subsections. The three repetitions describe observed variability, not confidence intervals. These are measurements of the original JAX CPU worker, not of Colab; no accuracy or efficiency-at-matched-error claim follows from this runtime-only experiment.
